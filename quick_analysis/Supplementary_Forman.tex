\section{Bochner Laplacian and Sheaves}
\label{sec:supplementary_bochner_sheaf}

We have seen in Section~\ref{sec:bochner} that, by construction, a Bochner
matrix is itself a graph sheaf Laplacian. Applying the Bochner decomposition to
the higher order sheaf Laplacian $H_k^{\mathcal F} = B_k^{\mathcal F} +
F_k^{\mathcal F}$, the resulting $B_k^{\mathcal F}$ is again a graph sheaf
Laplacian, but one living on the adjacency graph of the $k$-simplices, where
two $k$-simplices are adjacent when they are upper or lower adjacent in $X$.
Here we show, for $k=1$, that a Bochner Laplacian is in general not directly
the Hodge Sheaf Laplacian of $X$ itself.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:bochner_not_higher_order_sheaf}
The Bochner Laplacian of the simplicial complex
$X = \{\{0,1\},\{0,2\},\{0,3\},\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\}\}$ is not the Hodge Sheaf
Laplacian of any cellular sheaf of uniform stalk dimension defined on $X$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$X$ is the $1$-skeleton of the tetrahedron on $\{0,1,2,3\}$: it has no
$2$-simplices, so every edge is lower adjacent to the four edges it meets at a
vertex and upper adjacent to none. Let $H$ be the standard Hodge Laplacian of
$X$ for the trivial sheaf: orienting each edge $\{i,j\}$, $i<j$, from $i$ to
$j$, $H = \delta_0\delta_0^\top$ where $(\delta_0)_{e,w}=+1$ if $w$ is the
larger endpoint of $e$ and $-1$ if it is the smaller. Let $B$ be the Bochner
decomposition of $H$, so that $B_{ee}=4$ for every edge $e$ and, for edges
$e,e'$ sharing a vertex, $B_{ee'}=H_{ee'}=\pm1$, the sign fixed by the
orientation; $B_{ee'}=0$ if $e,e'$ are disjoint.

Suppose $B = H_1^{\mathcal F}$ for a sheaf $\mathcal F$ on $X$ with every stalk
of dimension $1$. Since $X$ has no $2$-simplices, the diagonal block at an edge
$e=(v,u)$ is $\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}^2 + \mathcal F_{u\trianglelefteq
e}^2$, and the entry between two edges $e,e'$ meeting only at $v$ is
$\pm\,\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e'}$, the sign
fixed by the orientation of $e,e'$ at $v$; therefore it should be the case that
$|\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}|\,|\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e'}| =1$.

Every vertex of $X$ meets exactly three edges. Fixing a vertex $v$ with
incident edges $e_1,e_2,e_3$ and writing $y_i := |\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq
e_i}|$, the three identities $y_1y_2=y_2y_3=y_1y_3=1$ force $y_1=y_2=y_3=1$.
This holds at every vertex, so $\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}^2=1$ for every
incident pair $(v,e)$, and the diagonal block forced by the sheaf is
$\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}^2+\mathcal F_{u\trianglelefteq e}^2=1+1=2$ at
every edge, contradicting $B_{ee}=4$. Hence no such $\mathcal F$ exists.
\end{proof}

Nevertheless, the relationship between the Bochner Laplacian and the notion of
orientation sheaf remains an open question.

\subsection{Global summary tables}

Tables~\ref{tab:global_general_other_norm}, \ref{tab:global_general_other_unnorm}, \ref{tab:global_cross_a} and \ref{tab:global_cross_b} give the global summaries, across all ten datasets, of the other-Laplacian ablation and cross-comparison of Section~\ref{sec:forman_profile}. The first two report the alignment of the \emph{other Laplacian}, that is, the same learnt restriction maps evaluated under the opposite normalisation from the one they were trained with, against the topological and zero references: as discussed in the main text, this alignment is lost entirely when normalised training is evaluated unnormalised, which rules out the reading that the learnt maps are simply close to the identity. The last two report the cross-comparison between the two training regimes, evaluated under matching normalisation, and show a moderate, sustained disagreement between them rather than either convergence or divergence.
